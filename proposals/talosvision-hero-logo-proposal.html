The Myth of Talos

Talos (Τάλως) was a giant automaton forged of bronze by Hephaestus, god of fire and metalwork. Gifted to King Minos of Crete, Talos' sole purpose was to guard the island's coastline — circling it three times each day, hurling boulders at approaching ships.

His single vein ran from neck to ankle, sealed with a bronze nail — his only vulnerability. Argonaut Medea exploited this, pulling the nail to drain his ichor, the divine fluid that animated him.

TalosVision inherits this legacy: an unblinking sentinel powered not by ichor but by intelligence. Where the bronze giant circled an island, our platform surveils perimeters. Where his eyes scanned the horizon, our machine vision detects, counts, and protects — always on, always watching, never resting.

"He was forged of bronze, and his duty was to protect. He is not a myth. He is a prototype."

How to Embed

Each geometry variation above is an inline SVG with a viewBox="0 0 200 200". Copy the SVG markup and place it anywhere in the main site HTML. All colors use currentColor-compatible hex values from the existing palette (#00D4FF, #3B82F6, #E8F4FD). Wrap in a container with controlled width for sizing.
